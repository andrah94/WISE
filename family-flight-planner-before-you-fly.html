Before a family flight, decide who is doing what

The bags may be packed, but a few departure jobs can still be floating around. Who has the travel documents? Who is checking the flight information? Has anyone confirmed the ride?

You can put those jobs on one page before everyone is standing at the door.

Give each job an owner

Write down the things that need doing and name the person handling each one. If you are the only adult traveling, use the same list to decide what you will do first and what can wait.

Children can help with tasks suited to their age. An adult should still make the final check of important items and arrangements.

Pack by person and by bag

A family packing list becomes easier to use when you can tell whose item it is and where it belongs. Separate what goes in each carry-on from what goes in other luggage.

For anything governed by travel rules, check current guidance from the airline, airport or relevant government agency. A planner organizes your choices; it does not replace those sources.

Keep the trip details together

Record the flight, transport and accommodation information you expect to need. Keep sensitive details private, especially if you share a page with someone helping you.

If an arrangement changes, update the plan rather than relying on an old message.

Do a short check the night before

Confirm where the documents are stored, review the latest flight information and check the ground transport arrangements. Then read through the departure jobs and make sure each person knows their part.
