What should be in the handoff before the next guest arrives?

A message saying “all done” can leave a few questions unanswered. Were the essentials restocked? Was anything damaged? Is there a loose handle that needs attention before someone arrives?

If you coordinate a vacation rental turnover with a cleaner, agree on what completion means before the work begins. A short written handoff gives both people something clear to work from.

Start with the property and the visit

Put the property, turnover date and person completing the check in the same place. If someone helps with more than one home, a note without that context can be hard to match to the right visit.

Keep the instructions specific to that property. What belongs in one home's kitchen may not belong in another.

Agree on the final checks

Choose a few things the host needs confirmed before the next arrival. That might include fresh linens, restocked essentials, any visible damage or maintenance concerns, and the photos you have agreed to receive.

These checks support the property's full cleaning instructions. They do not replace them.

If photos are part of the handoff, decide which views matter. Avoid including guests' belongings or personal information.

Give unfinished items somewhere to go

Suppose a lamp is not working. Record where it is, what was noticed and who will handle it. That is more useful than leaving the next person to discover it again.

Keep unresolved items visible rather than marking the whole turnover complete and hoping everyone remembers the conversation.
